Build a real lab instrument in one month

This is a hands-on short course for graduate students in experimental physics. In three two-hour workshops you learn the three skill sets needed to ship one small, genuinely useful device for a real lab: firmware and software written with an AI coding agent, an electronic circuit on a professionally assembled PCB, and an enclosure designed from a parametric CAD template and 3D-printed.

Alongside the workshops — about three hours a week for three weeks, ten hours of homework in total, and that is a promise — you build your part of the class instrument: one board, five blocks, five students, one repository. The board plugs onto an ESP32-S3 dev board and gives it what the dev board cannot do: 2 × ±10 V 16-bit waveform outputs, 8 × ±10 V 16-bit inputs, 4 relays, 2 isolated fast inputs, 8 TTL outputs and 2 fast trigger outputs, a switchable TRIG, a phone app, a Python API and an OLED — all from one USB-C cable. You route your block's zone of the PCB, review a classmate's, write your block's driver and app panel, and personalise the box. In the demo week (preliminary: 26–30 October) you give a 5-minute live demonstration from the class checklist — waveform out, seen on the scope and on the instrument's own input · change it from the phone · alarm → relay → notification · your block — with your 60-second video as the safety net.

The message of the course: AI lets a physicist operate at roughly 80 % of a competent electrical, mechanical and software engineer — but only if you can specify what you want and verify what you get. Specification and verification are the real skills we teach.

Before the first class, please complete the Preparation checklist (workbook ch. 0, at most one hour — not counted in the ten). You need to bring your own laptop; the dev board is handed to you at the door.

How the course works

One project, three lenses

The lectures are not separate topics — they are the three skills needed to ship one device: the class instrument. Every exercise is done once, on your own block of the one class board, and your block's panel and your box make it yours.

Workshop, not seminar

Each lecture runs as short demo → hands-on → debrief blocks, repeated, and a Claude tutor (/tutor in Claude Code) paces you through every hands-on step and the homework, in English or Mandarin. Theory appears only when it unblocks a decision ("what do ±10 V and 16 bits buy?").

AI writes, you verify

We use VS Code + Claude Code agentically inside the class repository. You learn the working practices — spec first, project conventions file, fresh sessions, handover notes, parallel agents — that make AI-assisted engineering reliable.

Do each step once, for real

Every step of shipping hardware is done once and for real — place the missing parts, download a part from LCSC, route your zone, open a PR, review a peer — and your zone goes into a professionally assembled board that you demo in a box. Ten hours of homework, maximum: cover less, well.
