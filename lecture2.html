Electronics 101 — add capability: from the dev board to the class board

Goal. Learn the six ideas needed to read our schematic, then do each step of making a PCB once, on your own block of the class board: place the few parts missing from your schematic sheet, route your zone in KiCad, get a clean DRC, open a pull request and review a classmate's. Your zone enters the class order on Monday 28 September.

Learning goals: the six ideas needed to read our schematic · place parts and route in KiCad · DRC · open and review a PR · understand why the class board exists (what the dev board cannot do).

Prerequisite: Homework 1 done — E2 merged, your block's two questions answered, KiCad 10 installed with the library package unzipped into the repo, and your SPEC.md paragraph.

The hardware model: dev board + carrier, assembled by JLCPCB

The MCU stays on the proven ESP32-S3 dev board; the class board is a carrier it plugs into — the analog front end done properly, isolation, buffers, robust connectors. One design, five units, one order. JLCPCB assembles everything, surface-mount and through-hole; the front panel is a second small PCB. You solder nothing, and the dev board alone keeps running the app as the permanent fallback. Fully assembled, the board costs about US$100 per student.

Topics — the six ideas, each pointed at our schematic PDF

(a) Rails, decoupling, ground

Where the power comes from and how it is cleaned up next to every chip; the one place analog and digital ground meet. Block B2.

(b) What ±10 V and 16 bits buy

Range and resolution on the input and output side, and why the dev board's own ADC and DAC are not enough. Blocks B1 and B3.

(c) The op-amp stage as a black box

Gain and offset without the internals: how the DAC's output becomes a ±10 V swing. Block B3.

(d) Isolation

Relay, opto-coupler, creepage distance: switching and sensing things that must not share your ground. Block B4.

(e) 3.3 V vs 5 V logic

Why a GPIO cannot drive 5 V TTL directly and what the buffers do. Block B5.

(f) The buses

SPI and I²C tie the blocks to the MCU; which pins carry the fastest signals in your block.

KiCad: the only six operations

Place a part and wire it · annotate · Update PCB from schematic · drag footprints into place · route · DRC (then the 3D view). Shown live once; V4 and V5 replay them.

Where parts come from

LCSC part numbers on every symbol; easyeda2kicad pulls symbol, footprint and 3D model; JLCPCB places what LCSC stocks.

PR + peer review

One PR for your zone, one review of a classmate's from the checklist, one round of fixes — the same list the instructor uses at merge.

Homework 2 (assessed, 3 h) — Exercises E7–E10

  • E7 (1.25 h) Finish routing. DRC 0 errors, 3D screenshot. Teaches: layout, ground pours, DRC discipline.
  • E8 (0.25 h) One part from LCSC. easyeda2kicad --full --lcsc_id C474881 (the 2-pin screw terminal; or your LED); add it to the project library; swap it in. Teaches: where parts come from, the LCSC → JLCPCB pipeline.
  • E9 (0.25 h) PR by Wednesday 23 September. Branch b<N>-<name>, commit, PR with the 3D screenshot; request your ring reviewer (A→B→C→D→E→A). Teaches: the one-repo workflow; your zone enters the class order.
  • E10 (0.75 h) Review your peer by Friday 25 September, fix yours by Sunday 27 September. Walk the checklist below on their PR; leave at least two comments; approve or request changes (0.5 h). Then address the comments on your own PR (0.25 h). Watch V5 (10 min).

Peer-review checklist (workbook ch. C — the same list the instructor uses at merge): ERC 0 / DRC 0 · LCSC field on every part · values match the PDF · decoupling next to the pins · nothing on layer 2 · nothing outside your rule area · pin 1 marked · ground pour joined · B4 creepage ≥ 2.5 mm · silkscreen label on every connector · CI green.

Gates: PRs Wed 23 Sep · reviews Fri 25 Sep (a public holiday — homework, not a session) · fixes Sun 27 Sep · Mon 28 Sep the instructor paste-merges the five zones into main, runs the full DRC, exports Gerbers/BOM/CPL and orders five assembled boards plus two bare boards and six front panels. A slip here is a slip everywhere; the week of 28 September carries no homework.
